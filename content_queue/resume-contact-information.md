# Resume Contact Information: What to Include and What to Skip

Your contact information should make it easy for an employer to reach you and verify your professional work. It should not include personal details that create privacy risk or distract from your qualifications. A small, accurate header is enough.

## Who this page is for

This guide is for you if:

- you are creating a resume for US internships or entry-level technical roles
- you are unsure whether to include an address, photo, birth date, or other personal details
- you want to format email, phone, location, and professional links clearly
- you need to make the header readable for people and applicant tracking systems

The examples below are illustrative. Use contact channels you check regularly.

## Essential contact details

Most technical resumes need:

- Full name
- Professional email address
- Reliable phone number
- City and state, or another clear general location when relevant
- One or two professional links, such as LinkedIn, GitHub, or a portfolio

You do not need a full street address for most applications. City and state usually provide enough location context while protecting privacy.

Use the same name across your resume, LinkedIn, portfolio, and application form where practical. Consistency makes your documents easier to match.

## Use a professional email address

A simple address based on your name is easiest to recognize:

- firstname.lastname@example.com
- firstname.lastname+jobs@example.com

A university email can work while it is active, but make sure you will still have access after graduation. A personal professional address is often more durable.

Avoid nicknames, jokes, old gaming handles, and addresses that are difficult to read aloud. Check the account regularly and make sure employer messages do not disappear into spam.

## Format phone and location clearly

Use the standard format appropriate to your number:

- (555) 010-2030
- +1 555 010 2030 for international presentation

Set up a professional voicemail greeting and check that the mailbox works. A missed call should not lead to a full inbox or an informal message.

For location, use a general area such as Austin, TX, or New York, NY. If the role is remote or you are relocating, explain it in the application or a concise relevant line rather than using a full home address.

## Include only useful professional links

A good link gives the reviewer additional evidence:

- LinkedIn with complete education, role target, and project details
- GitHub with pinned repositories and useful READMEs
- A portfolio with working demos, case studies, or reports
- A personal site with professional writing or technical documentation

Do not add every social account. Casual profiles that contain unrelated or unprofessional content do not help.

Use a clean profile URL when possible. Test every link in a private browser window to make sure it works without a login and points to the intended profile.

## Personal details to skip in US applications

For most US technical resumes, leave out:

- Date of birth or age
- Social Security number or other government identifiers
- Marital status and family details
- Religion, nationality, or immigration details unless specifically required by law or the process
- Full street address
- Personal photograph in most technology applications
- Unrelated social media handles
- Salary history or expectations in the contact header

If an employer needs work authorization or other administrative information, provide it through the appropriate application channel rather than the resume header.

## Keep the header parseable

Place essential contact details in the main document body, not only inside a decorative header, footer, image, or text box. Some parsing workflows can miss content in those areas.

A simple format works well:

- Taylor Morgan  
- Austin, TX | taylor.morgan@example.com | (555) 010-2030  
- linkedin.com/in/taylormorgan | github.com/taylormorgan

Avoid icon-only email, phone, and location labels. Symbols can turn into unreadable characters during export. Plain text is easier to understand and parse.

## Choose a professional display name

Use the name you professionally go by, with a clear legal-name match in the application if required. If you use a preferred name, keep it consistent across professional profiles.

Do not include a nickname that makes the document difficult to match with your accounts. Avoid adding a target job title to your name if it makes you appear to hold a position you have not earned. A separate summary can state your target more accurately.

## Review public profile consistency

Before adding LinkedIn or GitHub, compare the profile with the resume:

- Degree and graduation dates match
- Project names and contributions match
- Current target does not contradict the resume
- Public repositories are accessible
- No placeholder biography, broken demo, or private information is visible

A weak public profile can make the application less clear. Improve the profile first or omit the link until it supports your evidence.

## Common contact information mistakes

- Using an outdated email or a phone with no working voicemail.
- Including a full home address and unnecessary personal details.
- Adding every social media profile.
- Hiding contact information inside an image or decorative header.
- Using icons without text labels.
- Sending links to private or unfinished profiles.
- Providing different names and graduation dates across documents.

## Copy-ready contact header

**[Full Name]**  
[City, State] | [Professional email] | [Phone number]  
[LinkedIn URL] | [GitHub or portfolio URL]

For an international application:

**[Full Name]**  
[City, Country] | [Email] | [International phone format]  
[Professional profile or portfolio]

Use only the lines you actually need. One strong project link is better than four unrelated profiles.

## Adapt the header to four common situations

**Current student**

- Maya Rodriguez | Austin, TX
- maya.rodriguez@example.edu | (555) 010-2030
- github.com/mayar

**Recent graduate with a durable personal email**

- Maya Rodriguez | Seattle, WA
- maya.rodriguez@example.com | (555) 010-2030
- linkedin.com/in/mayarodriguez | mayarodriguez.dev

**Candidate applying from another country**

- Maya Rodriguez | Toronto, Canada
- maya.rodriguez@example.com | +1 555 010 2030
- linkedin.com/in/mayarodriguez

**Candidate with one strong project profile and no LinkedIn**

- Maya Rodriguez | maya.rodriguez@example.com | (555) 010-2030
- github.com/mayar

Each version includes only information that helps contact or verify the candidate. Do not add relocation explanations, work authorization details, or multiple social profiles to the header unless the employer needs them. Those topics usually belong in the application form or interview.

## Protect privacy while staying accessible

A professional header should work for an employer without making your personal life public. Use a professional email and general location, and keep government identifiers, home addresses, and private account details out of the document.

Check the metadata and public links too. A document property, profile page, or repository can reveal information even when the header looks clean. Remove old phone numbers, private email addresses, and personal website analytics or admin links.

Accessibility also means reliability. If you change your phone number or email, update the resume, application profiles, LinkedIn, and portfolio before applying again. An employer should never have to guess which channel is current.

## Handle remote work and relocation clearly

For a remote role, use your current general location and make sure it matches the application form. If you are planning a move, do not list the future city as if you already live there. Discuss timing in the appropriate application field or interview when it affects eligibility.

A short relocation note can be useful when the employer requires office attendance. Keep it factual and place it in a cover letter or form rather than crowding the contact header.

## Include international context only when useful

For international applications, use a phone format that includes the country code and an email you can access across time zones. There is no need to list your time zone in the header unless the employer specifically asks for scheduling information.

## Keep a current copy for each application

Before every submission, compare the header with the contact fields in the application form and correct any mismatch immediately.

## Final contact review

Send yourself a test email and call the listed number. Open every profile link without signing in, and compare the public details with your resume.

Remove personal information that does not help the employer evaluate you. Finally, copy the resume into a plain-text editor to make sure your name, email, phone, and links remain readable in the correct order.

## FAQ

### Should I include my full address on a resume?

Usually not. City and state or city and country are enough for many applications. Provide a full address only when the employer specifically needs it through an appropriate process.

### Is a university email acceptable?

Yes, while you can access it reliably. Consider using a durable personal professional address if the university account will close after graduation.

### How many professional links should I include?

One or two useful links are usually enough. Choose the profiles that best verify your projects and professional identity.

### Can I put contact details in the page header?

Keep essential details in the main document body as well. Decorative headers and text boxes can be skipped or scrambled in some parsing workflows.
